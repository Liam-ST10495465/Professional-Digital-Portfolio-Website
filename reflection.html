Professional Reflection

01

What have I learned during this module?

This module gave me a much clearer picture of what it means to work as an IT professional rather than just someone who can write code. Building this portfolio taught me practical skills like structuring a multi-page website with consistent navigation, applying basic SEO principles such as descriptive titles and alt text, and making sure a site works properly on both desktop and mobile. Beyond the technical side, it also reinforced the importance of presenting your work clearly, something that matters just as much in a professional setting as the code itself.

02

Which IT professional skill is currently my strongest?

My strongest skill right now is logical problem-solving, particularly when working in Java. I'm comfortable breaking a larger task down into smaller steps, working through the logic methodically, and testing each part as I go rather than trying to write an entire solution at once. This structured approach has served me well in both programming exercises and in planning out a project like this portfolio site.

03

Which skill would I like to improve?

I'd like to improve my use of version control, specifically Git and GitHub. I understand the basic workflow of committing and pushing changes, but I haven't yet built the habit of using branches, writing clear commit messages, or collaborating with others through pull requests. These are all things that matter a great deal in real development teams.

04

What will I do to develop that skill?

To build this up, I plan to use Git more deliberately in future projects, creating separate branches for new features, writing meaningful commit messages instead of generic ones, and practising how to resolve merge conflicts. I'll also look into contributing to a small open-source project once I'm more confident, as a way to practise working within someone else's codebase and review process.

05

How could this portfolio support my future studies and career?

I intend to treat this portfolio as a living document, updating it each semester as I complete new modules, projects, and certifications. Having a public, well-organised record of my work gives me something concrete to share with potential employers or link on LinkedIn, and building it has already forced me to get better at describing my own work clearly and concisely, a skill that will matter in interviews and throughout my career.
